Notes for Steve

How the panel is organised, and what to look at. Everything is clickable.

1. Looks are presets, like every module

Film Look's complete recipes open from the same Preset button and the same Adjustment Presets dialog as Detail, Vignette and the rest: Apply, Delete, Save current. Built-in recipes sit under "Built-in", yours under "Saved". One dialog keeps editing, deleting and importing in one place for all modules.

Try: Preset in the Film Look header, then Apply Night Tungsten.

Under Look Strength, the Film stock card says what the stock brings, in plain words.

2. Five stages, in the order light travels

Camera → Negative → Development → Print → Scan. Each folds open with a small triangle and has no number, so it never reads as a module. When folded, it shows a short summary (for example "35mm · Pro-Mist 1/4") and how many controls you changed.

Try: fold Negative and open Print.

3. "From the stock" or set

Values the stock fills in are grey and marked STOCK. Once you change one, its value turns white, its slider fills violet, and a dot appears by its name. Clicking the dot puts the stock's value back.

Try: move Grain Amount, then switch the stock to Portra 800. Your grain stays; halation follows the new stock. Then click the dot.

4. Stage shortcuts

Small buttons at the top of a stage (Lens, Format, Print, Scan) change that stage only, and what they change shows dots. They are fixed built-in shortcuts, not managed presets: see Q1.

5. Slides

Pick Velvia or Kodachrome: Print fades out with a short note, and the Print summary says "No print".

6. Output is a note, not a stage

There is nothing to set; it just tells you what happens to HDR highlights. In SDR, "Match HDR film look" sits at the top.

Changes from the plan

  • Lens is called Camera and also holds Film Format and Film Exposure: these are camera decisions.
  • The stock picker sits above the stages, not inside Negative, because it drives all of them.
  • Push/Pull is five buttons (−2 to +2) instead of a slider: it only takes whole stops.
  • Grain response trims sit in the Grain tile under "Tone response".

Questions for you

Q1. Stage shortcuts. Keep them as fixed buttons inside each stage (quick, but you cannot add, edit or delete them), or move them into the shared Preset dialog as sections ("Camera", "Print"...), where they could be managed like any preset?
Q2. Which stage starts open? The app's rule is that groups start closed. Inside Film Look I'd open Negative by default, since it holds grain and halation. Or keep all five closed.
Q3. "STOCK" tag. Is the small grey tag next to inherited values helpful, or noise? Without it, only the grey value tells you.
