# Grain size and colour in classical Denoise

November 20, 2026 · MacBook Neo, 8 GB and 6 cores · research only.

The shape of the grain is set mainly by which wavelet bands survive. Demosaicing has already made nearby pixels resemble each other by the time those bands are measured. On the Canon dark background, Fine Grain and Clean Grain leave a median wavelength of about 8 native pixels. On the Sony flats it is about 15–16 pixels, at much lower visible contrast. At matched brightness noise, keeping a little more of the finest band and less of the broad bands brought the Canon dark background down to about 4 pixels. That trial produced block-like edges around the flame and worsened some marked spots. Half-strength guided colour limited to quiet shadows is the candidate worth carrying forward. It reduced Canon shadow mottling by 11–12% and met the marked-spot and small-light screens here.

## What DxO has disclosed

- **HQ and Standard.** DxO’s current PhotoLab guide says Standard also works on TIFF and JPEG. On RAW, the default strength is tuned to the camera body and ISO. Standard has separate controls for chrominance specks, low-frequency grain clumps and RAW-only maze and dead-pixel artifacts. The Optics Pro 9 manual already showed the separate chrominance and Low Frequency controls. This points to intentional treatment of colour and grain scale independent of the luminance setting. The RGB support establishes a developed-image path. The exact treatment order, colour space and filter for current Standard RAW are undisclosed. Check the app version and selected mode before comparing, since current PureRAW documentation focuses on newer modes and older PureRAW versions offered different choices. [PhotoLab guide](https://userguides.dxo.com/en/photolab/the-customize-tab) · [Optics Pro 9 manual, pp. 33–35](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/lit_files/97226.pdf) · [DxO on HQ file support](https://support.dxo.com/hc/en-us/articles/9801878988317-Can-I-also-use-DxO-PurewRAW-with-my-JPEGs-and-TIFFs) · [PureRAW guide](https://userguides.dxo.com/en/pureraw/processing-tools-and-settings)
- **A documented classical RAW path.** In 2007 DxO said Optics Pro 5 applied noise reduction before demosaicing, specifically describing fine, even residual grain. Chief scientist Frédéric Guichard described a separate nonlocal demosaicer that used evidence farther away than immediate neighbours. This work shipped years before PRIME and establishes an early classical RAW path. Details of the current HQ filter remain private. [DxO’s Optics Pro 5 release](https://www.imaging-resource.com/ir-archive/news-archive/dxo-optics-pro-v5-released/) · [Guichard’s briefing and DxO announcement](https://www.dpreview.com/articles/3672755571/dxo5/)
- **Classical PRIME.** Guichard’s 2013 launch briefing described looking at roughly a thousand neighbours per pixel to find similar image structure in RAW. The researchers behind non-local means and NL-Bayes say their group's work on both denoising and self-similarity demosaicing was used in DxO software, without naming modes or versions. Patch-based estimation is consequently a strong inference for PRIME. Its particular estimator, the exact stage at which patch weights were calculated and the method of colour separation remain open. DPReview independently compared 100% crops of HQ and PRIME and reported PRIME as pre-demosaic. DxO’s own transition announcement describes classical processing as separate operations. Together the sources support RAW-domain work with separate denoise and demosaic steps, with the exact legacy PRIME handoff still undocumented by DxO. The 2013 DxO video is a product demonstration. The claim about the search comes from the named chief scientist’s launch statement. [DxO’s PRIME release](https://www.dpreview.com/articles/4628540370/dxo-labs-introduces-optics-pro-9/) · [DxO’s Optics Pro 9 demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cJT9wqOvcyA) · [TAMI research group and papers](https://blocs.uib.cat/tami/projects/denoising-and-restoration-of-digital-images/) · [DPReview’s PhotoLab 4 review](https://www.dpreview.com/reviews/9542415704/dxo-photolab-4-software-review/) · [DxO’s account of the transition](https://www.prweb.com/releases/dxo-photolab-4-revolutionary-in-every-respect-882806776.html)
- **A particularly relevant patent.** DxO’s EP2174289 describes denoising the Bayer mosaic, demosaicing the quiet mosaic, and optionally adding back some of the removed RAW residual as fine grey grain. The add-back is pixel-local, so demosaicing never spreads that residual into coloured clumps. It describes several options, including retaining RAW noise level and grain attributes. The earlier EP1410331 family describes device settings and calibration in image processing. DxO researchers Buzzi and Guichard measured how the imaging chain reshapes noise correlations. Buades and colleagues’ 2009 paper describes using image self-similarity for classical demosaicing. These are complementary evidence and give a plausible explanation for the look Steve saw. A patent example leaves open whether and where today’s Standard or older PRIME uses it. [EP2174289A2, Fig. 2](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP2174289A2/en) · [EP1410331B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1410331B1/en) · [Buzzi and Guichard, ICIP 2005](https://corp.dxomark.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/2005_Noise_Measure_ICIP2005.pdf) · [Buades et al., IEEE TIP 2009](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIP.2009.2017171)

The best explanation for the **Standard/HQ** look Steve described is camera-calibrated separation of fine brightness grain, coarse clumps and colour, potentially assisted by earlier cleanup on the RAW path. A stronger RAW patch search specifically fits the evidence for **PRIME**. How much either contributes to Steve’s photo needs matched exports because DxO’s tone, rendering profile and lens sharpening also change how large and colourful the same noise looks.

## How the comparison was made

The manifest verified `IMG_0790.CR2` (Canon 7D, ISO 3200) and `_DSC7970.ARW` (Sony A7R III, ISO 6400). Both used the existing harness’s native signed AHD development, lens correction off, no geometry resampling and Even out harsh specks enabled at 2.5. The adaptive noise model was measured on the full source via the shipping bounded estimator. Both exact 18 px two-band ratios came from the neighbouring GPU study with identical model and controls. The radius-6 comparison refit both gains on each whole photo. Standard, Fine Grain and Clean Grain Small use their defaults in all reference columns. The test applies the production CPU reconstruction math to padded native areas. Clean is full precision in these trials. The shipped preview stores its colour correction in half precision. [Harness](../../../../codebase/tools/denoise_eval/README.md) · [Denoise implementation](../../denoising.md) · [GPU study](../../briefs/denoise-grain-gpu-design-2026-11-20.md)

All flat patches are 256 × 256 at native position. Their brightness labels are relative to each photo. The Canon midtone is a softly lit floor patch, and the Sony midtone is the grey backdrop. The Canon torso patch was retained as a texture control and excluded from noise conclusions after the scout showed real structure. Additional checks used a second Canon dark patch and the Sony dark shirt and second backdrop patch. Real-photo measurements are estimates of residual noise plus any remaining scene variation. A generated neutral Bayer flat gives an actual noise-only check.

For brightness noise, remove a Gaussian local trend (14 px sigma), subtract the weighted mean and measure on a Hann window. Report median **power wavelength** in native px over 1/48–1/2 cycles per pixel and one-pixel autocorrelation averaged across the two axes. Wavelength is an indicator of apparent size. Use root-mean-square (RMS) power of both opponent-colour axes in the 8–32 px band for blotches. The matched trials target the same pooled scene-Y RMS as Clean Grain, normalizing each fit window to its own baseline. I used ±5% per flat window as a descriptive match allowance so coarser grain cannot win by being quieter. Colour is the separate measured outcome. The originals at default settings establish a reference at different levels of noise removal.

Mark peaks were located once from Standard, within the existing harness’s local search around each of Steve’s 38 Canon and 25 Sony marks. All outputs were then read at the same location with a fixed lift for each photo. The ring-based thresholds use Clean Grain’s original grain level. A material worsening means a rise of more than 25% and more than two 8-bit display levels in the original local contrast, with new crossings of twice the original ring grain tracked separately. For the real railing light and synthetic light, the descriptive allowance was 1% in scene-Y peak and local excess light, small enough to flag a repeat of the prior light loss. All displayed detail measures use a fixed mask from Clean Grain and measure signed texture or edge contrast in the same direction. They are relative retention measures on noisy originals.

All size columns are in native px. Standard acutely suppresses fine variation in these samples. On the Canon dark patch its scene-Y RMS is .000162, versus .001052 in Fine and Clean. Its coarser residual is much quieter. Sony grey gives .000475 in Standard and .000534 in Fine and Clean.

1. **Band weighting sets the output shape.** On Canon dark, the retained achromatic wavelet bands 0–2 have RMS of .000006, .001326 and .000717 in the code’s opponent component. On Sony grey, those values are .000073, .000240 and .000425. In both, the finest band contributes little beside the medium bands. At matched RMS, adding back some existing fine coefficients and reducing broad ones lowered Canon dark lag-one correlation from .80 to .41. Sony grey moves from .93 to .89. The scale shift is stronger on the Canon than on the Sony.
2. **18 pixels is the reach for averaging the amount kept.** This is a gain map, so the underlying band still determines the scale of the brightness pattern. The Canon fit adds a near-constant gain floor of .81 and .93 in bands 1 and 2, much more than Sony’s .05 and .23, because the global fit preserves energy including the bright parts of the photo. Radius 6 changes the Canon dark RMS from .00105 to .00063 but leaves its wavelength near 8 px. On Sony the change slightly coarsens the residual and lowers RMS. The fit redistributes how much grain remains and which band dominates locally. The averaging reach alone cannot deliver the fine texture.
3. **Demosaic creates the initial correlation.** With independent generated Bayer noise, one-pixel correlation was .004 at the sensor, .26 after AHD in scene-Y weights and .63 for the two opponent-colour axes. Median wavelength went from 2.84 px in the mosaic to 3.31 in brightness and 6.06 in colour. In the real flat windows, adjacent same-phase RAW samples two native pixels apart correlate between −.014 and .033 before development. The developed real scene-Y lag-one correlation is .31–.33. The synthetic check isolates the interpolation from scene content and the RAW clamp. The native inputs show the same direction after development.
4. **Colour changes apparent brightness in some situations.** Standard’s nominal brightness axis is an equal RGB sum. The true ACEScg scene luminance is weighted toward green, so independent shrinking of opponent channels can also change scene Y. Clean Grain explicitly compensates for that after Fine Grain, and its scene-Y spectra are identical here to two decimals. The lifted display still maps signed shadows, deep orange and clipped colours nonlinearly. After holding scene Y and comparing to a neutral render at the same Y, the colour-related display-Y RMS on the Canon dark patch measures roughly 3 code values, against 38 for Clean's whole display-Y residual at +6 EV. Marked spots and the fire show more visible local changes than that flat average.

## Why colour mottling survives

The classically demosaiced source carries correlated colour. The wavelet estimator compares each band’s local energy with its predicted noise. A broad or unusually strong colour change can look like structure and get retained. Clean Grain’s relative-colour pass protects recurring one-sided colour, real texture and bright edges. Distributed, low-contrast mottling can fall below its local significance check, and a wide pull is intentionally weak near flame. The floor also illustrates how a local repair can reduce small extremes and still leave more mid-scale opponent energy. Its 8–32 px RMS rises from .000441 in Fine to .000751 in Clean, although the dark background falls from .000205 to .000124. Sony grey is already heavily cleaned by Clean, from .000758 in Fine to .000256. More smoothing has little left to gain there.

A luma-only guide misses boundaries between colours of the same brightness. The two colour trials used a fixed brightness guide and two exposure-relative opponent guides to recognize those boundaries. Both add a signed colour correction with a luminance compensation. The existing harsh-speck step remains in the RAW path, and the research filters independently exclude protected bright features. Earlier tests showed why both protections matter. Broad averaging bled into coloured text, stars and lamp edges. Local healing also made some blue and cyan marks more obvious by changing the surrounding brightness. ## For Steve

The grain looks coarse because the RAW conversion has already linked neighbouring pixels, then Denoise removes more of the very finest grain and leaves some of the larger patterns. Fine Grain’s 18-pixel setting evens out how much of those patterns is left. A smaller setting had much more effect on how loud the grain was than on its size.

DxO Standard most likely gets part of its look by treating coarse clumps and colour separately from fine brightness grain, with settings calibrated to the camera. DxO also documented cleaning RAW noise before it could spread and patented a way to put some fine grey grain back. A same-photo DxO export would show how much the difference matters on these pictures.

The best candidate here is **half-strength guided colour in quiet shadows**. It slightly cleans up the Canon dark background and suits tile-by-tile GPU processing around the visible area at zoom. It passed the mark and small-light screens used here. The brighter patches and the Sony gained little. The more ambitious fine-grain recipe made visible artifacts around the fire and face.
