# Film Look responsiveness research — MacBook Neo

**Make the same faster Film in the editor and in delivery.** Dropping Grain only until release leaves the exact-frame bottleneck in place and can make the picture visibly change. The real cost comes from two places: rebuilding a 27-part Mist glow after an Exposure edit, and reconstructing physical Grain at up to 16 samples for every Fit pixel after release. Halation is not the main bottleneck. The stock/print colour and scan do not need rebuilding first to reach the target.

On a 24 MP Portra 400 + Black Pro-Mist + Halation + Grain + Scan grade, Film off fed back in 38 ms. With Film on, a sampled Exposure drag produced a GPU-completed candidate after ~200 ms, but only a 1078 ms frame was still current at the audit's next-animation check. On the same drag the release took ~790 ms. Individual samples were noisy, but the GPU breakdown is clear: **108–134 ms in Mist plus 42–52 ms in draft finish per drag frame**. The release reused Mist in this sample and then spent **552 ms in the full Grain/stock finish**. Lens, Halation and Grain control drags that could reuse Mist paid mostly for Grain and the generation check. Print Contrast has a separate table download/generation stall.

**Recommendation.** First pair a faster, visually matched Grain field with calibrated multi-scale Mist, applied consistently in Direct, Tiled, Proof and CPU/GPU export. Anchor image coordinates and random seed and filter Grain to the pixel footprint rather than giving drag and release different sample budgets. Start with eight fitted coloured Mist lobes on the existing grid (the fixed calibration below is safer than fitting each zoom separately). Preserve narrow lobes and only move broad lobes to cheaper levels if the compact fit is still too slow. Keep Halation, stock/print and scan as they are initially. Parallel changes to the Print Contrast data path and scheduler should cut the delays the GPU changes cannot remove. Do not re-run the whole film chain solely because pointer-up changed the tier label if the exact same finished frame is already valid.

The first lab gate is both Fit photos with Film alone, then Film + Detail + Denoise, under the same GPU lock and presentation conditions. Aim for Film work under ~35–50 ms/frame to leave input, scheduling and other modules headroom. Accept only measured **≤100 ms additional first feedback and settle** on both cases, plus a pixel check that toggling the interaction flag did not alter the current picture. A planning estimate for the rebuilt Film chain alone is ~30–65 ms at Canon Fit and ~30–70 ms at Sony Fit (including ~15–35 ms budget for Mist misses). That implies roughly 70–120 ms input-to-first frame on an otherwise light warm grade if a frame survives the current-generation check, and ~30–100 ms added release latency with at most one extra completed render. These are design targets, not measured gains. No measured guarantee yet on 50 MP post-fix Fit, 100% zoom, full delivery or combined Grade. Before choosing to change the default look, Steve should compare blinded crops of fine Grain in flat areas and faces, specular halos around hard edges and dark lines, and mixed-colour highlights. The trade-off is a different but deliberately matched artistic texture and bloom on existing grades, not a picture that changes on mouse-up.

## Cheaper preset decision, and Neon cross-check

**If the goal is a fast default now, make both heavy effects opt-in on new fast presets.** Keep them manually available, label their cost, and leave already authored grades and user-saved Looks alone. Removing either effect by itself does not reliably reach both Fit targets for a preset that uses the other. Presets already without lens diffusion only need Grain left out to get this main benefit. This is low effort compared with shader and CPU/Proof/export rework, but it changes what those new presets look like.

| Leave out of the preset | Would it hit both targets alone? | Visible result and effort |
|---|---|---|
| Film Grain | **No for a preset keeping Mist.** It removes the ~0.5 s settle cost, but the same Exposure edit still pays 108–134 ms for Mist alone before other work. A preset with no Mist is a good candidate for Grain opt-in as its only change. | Smoother flat areas, shadows and skin, no intentional analog texture. Low effort in recipe/default policy and warning with same applied output everywhere. A replacement that keeps the texture needs CPU/GPU and zoom matching, much more effort. |
| Lens diffusion / Mist | **No if physical Grain stays.** Drag should improve substantially, but the ~0.5 s exact Grain finish remains on release. | Crisper edges and highlights with less veil and halo. Low effort in preset fields and warning, far less than recreating a matching pyramid in all routes. Lens None is already the underlying default, so only explicitly Mist-enabled presets need changing. |
| Both, keep stock/print colour and Halation | **Most likely to pass on a warmed Tone edit. Not directly tested.** The expensive passes are gone, with Halation and fixed scan/copies in the single-digit range before stock; cold Print Contrast still needs the separate fix. | Cleaner, crisper, grain-free grade retains the stock's colour/tone and the separately authored red Halation. No release swap, no impact on old renders. Quickest option to test/ship if Steve accepts the new preset look. Does not help Steve's manually enabled heavy Film edits. |
| Halation or Scan Sharpening | **No.** Halation's three passes were ~2.5–3.3 ms in the timed recipe, and scan ~2–3 ms. | Lose red rim glow or scan crispness for little speed. Not worth dropping on performance grounds at Fit. |

Neon's three claims against the evidence:

- **Grain at settle: confirmed in scale.** At this Fit footprint, \`count=4\` and the 16-sample cap yields 16 samples. The complete post-Mist finish cost about 490–552 ms versus ~33–52 ms with one sample. It also applies halation and stock, so quote ~0.5 s for the finish dominated by Grain, not a separately measured pure-Grain duration. On a different saved real grade the November 16 ablation measured median GPU 337 ms Grain-on vs 13.5 ms off at 1024 Fit.
- **Mist: 54 Gaussian passes confirmed, but the quoted 150–250 ms overstates this measured GPU stage.** Add one weights and one extraction pass, plus an optional cache copy. The 24 MP Exposure-drag misses were **108–134 ms Mist** in five traced frames; the full GPU command buffers completed in **166–201 ms**. Do not apply those as the timings of a cache hit, Sony, extreme lens sizes, or 100% zoom.
- **Print Contrast: confirmed for each uncached exact combination actually sent to the renderer; not each raw pointer event.** Same or in-flight keys return without another request, scheduler coalescing can skip intermediate values, and the 8-slot variant store can evict older keys. On a clean miss, the path awaits Python's response before drawing. The audited Portra requests measured ~65 ms backend construction/serialization and 4.38 MB JSON, before parse/pack/upload. My first traced Print submission came ~94 ms after input, vs ~19 ms for warm Exposure. This supports an exact data-path fix independent of any colour LUT.

### Why first feedback can be nearly a second

The audit measures input → accepted GPU fence → next animation callback, and only counts frames that still match the latest accepted generation. It does not observe display photons. When 166–201 ms GPU command buffers overlap faster input, many are already out of date by that check. The normal scheduler coalesces around one frame in flight, maintains a pending latest frame, and defers settles when an interactive frame is in flight. GPU admission/presentation also checks generations. Moving from generation-based queue acceptance to actual frame survival is preferable to claiming early frames were seen. Capture screen/compositor behavior separately before changing the metric or allowing stale images to publish. Only cancel work that has not reached the GPU, and never let a stale frame replace a newer frame. The earlier O1-5 scheduling change is held because the parity capture saw a competing app frame, despite matching completed pixels. Fix the comparability issue before trusting that approach.

Warm Exposure's first traced submit was ~19 ms after the input, so most of that first ~200 ms was GPU, not JS bind groups or compilation. Own-control trace example: warm Lens starts submitting at ~22 ms; its first GPU fence + callback at ~78 ms is then marked stale. Print submitted only at ~94 ms in its traced drag. That is consistent with separately measured M5-12 (~65 ms backend table work and 4.38 MB JSON per missed Portra contrast recipe), independent of GPU work. Direct warm format pipelines are cached and no general GPU readback is needed just to draw this HDR case. Avoid using cold proxy preparation and scope draining to explain the warm drag numbers. Sum only non-overlapping pass-end deltas; some Metal pass beginning timestamps are effectively shared, so individual start-to-end intervals overlap and cannot be summed.

### Measured optical approximation, without using the GPU

I fit 4 and 6 shared Gaussian lobes with non-negative RGB weights to the existing 27-lobe centre-of-frame point response (lens size 1, 35 mm fill, same truncation cap). The CPU calculation matches cumulative light at 17 radii, not photographs, not edges, not actual GPU throughput. On the 24 and 50 MP Fit geometries, six lobes keep the worst sampled encircled-light error between **0.17 and 0.78 percentage points** across Glimmerglass, Black Pro-Mist, Pro-Mist and CineBloom. On the 50 MP native grid, the six-lobe worst errors are **0.45–0.63 pp**. Four lobes are less faithful (~1.01–2.19 pp Fit, up to 3.08 pp native). The six-lobe Black Pro-Mist candidate cuts the algorithmic per-axis taps from 1391 to 220 (Canon Fit), 1121 to 186 (Sony Fit), and 1897 to 264 (Sony native grid). It would cut the blur dispatches from 54 to 12 even before a pyramid. This makes a **six-lobe fit on the existing grid the lower-effort Mist trial**. A broad-only pyramid can follow if it still misses the budget. Do not use the 83–86% tap reduction as an 83–86% wall-time claim.

At Fit, **20–25% of Black Pro-Mist's scattered energy** is in lobes narrower than σ = 1 pixel, and Glimmerglass has ~58%. A simple quarter grid for the whole blur is not safely invisible. Its present four-point source gather also samples only four of sixteen source pixels at grid 4, so fine point sources can be missed depending on their location. Retain the tight core in sharp source/full or half-resolution, use an area-filtered source pyramid, and qualify near-edge response, point-source brightness and RGB halo shape before moving wide lobes to lower levels. The fit should be calibrated once across the whole size/gate scale continuum, not selected anew by photo or preview mode. The radial calculation and all per-lens results are in `sol-psf.py` and `sol-psf.txt`.

A second CPU fit used one fixed set of physical radii and RGB weights **per lens**, shared across both Fit geometries and 50 MP native at four Size settings, then checked at additional combinations of Super 35 / Super 16 / 35mm and size. Eight lobes were materially better matched than six: worst sampled held-out encircled-light errors were **0.24–0.54 pp** across all four lenses (six lobes: 0.52–1.62 pp). Eight still cut 54 blur passes to **16**, with no pyramid allocator redesign required. Try this version first for the colour and tight-core risk. These are ideal centre point-spread fits, not a guarantee of invisibility or measured speed. Sources: `sol-psf-shared.py` and `sol-psf-shared.txt`.

### Export and 100% zoom are different speed tests

Both changes should cut Film work in delivery and at 100% but not by the Fit ratios. Export already often runs Mist on a quarter-resolution grid, so changing the Fit scale to quarter alone does not make native export faster. Reducing the lobe count and keeping broad blur out of giant per-tile overlap is where the native GPU/CPU wins may lie. Prove global pyramid alignment and memory use on the 8 GB Mac before adopting a whole-frame pyramid. Likewise native grain usually already uses one physical sample, so a faster field replaces the substantial single-sample density computation rather than removing 15 samples. A native 1:1 crop is where differences in speck shape and edge glow will be easiest to see. No seconds or multiplier for export or 100% are supported by these Fit traces. Export includes decode, other grading, readback and encoding, so qualify action-to-file separately for 24 and 50 MP and the default CPU fallback. Compare a downsampled export to Fit at the same viewing scale, then compare 100% to native export. “Same look” does not require comparing individual Fit pixels with unscaled native export pixels.
