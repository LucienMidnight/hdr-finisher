# Where Fine Grain and Clean Grain delivery spend the wait

November 20, 2026 · MacBook Neo · HDR Finisher 0.9.3, `feat/denoise-v1`

**Start by sharing the validated JPEG between export and full-size Proof.** On an unchanged grade, the second action can use the file the first already made. CPU Proof currently renders both renditions and builds the JPEG again, then builds two comparison pictures. The GPU trial already shares the rendered HDR and SDR pair with Proof, then encodes it again. Sharing the JPEG removes both repeats on the CPU and still saves the second encode on the trial. The cold delivery work worth tackling next is the JPEG highlight-ceiling retry loop.

- On the Canon CPU Proofs, **18–25 s** went into encoding and validating the JPEG, and **13–14 s** went into preparing and showing the two comparison views. The Canon fit and tile work took **3–10 s** depending on method and size. Sony Clean Grain was heavier at about **16 s** for the fit and tiles before its interrupted export.
- Canon Clean Grain Large took **46.91 s to export** and **59.95 s to Proof**. Its Proof rebuilt an identical JPEG with four more encode/check passes. Reusing the first JPEG would remove about **45 s**, or **76%** of that Proof wait. The comparable estimate for Canon Fine and Clean Small is about **70–75%**.
- The **10 minute 10 second** automatic Canon Proof review peaked at **1.96 GB**. Repeated builds caused separate macOS pressure stops. Sony's GPU trial asks for **at least 5.33 GB** under its admission formula, so it goes straight to the CPU.

The CPU delivery path estimates or looks up the **noise model**, then **fits the grain** over the whole photo and resolves all the tiles. Those are different jobs. Both CPU renditions shared the same denoised image in these runs because the RAW and Denoise controls were the same. They still ran separate finishing passes. The CPU delivery resolves Grain into a native RGB array. Its export scratch is mainly file-backed *finished* HDR/SDR strips when the admission check selects them, plus the encoder's own disk files. The Grade-only `GrainPreparation` scratch and its writer-lock timings from the earlier audit describe a different path. Writes through the mapped strips occur during rendering. The trace measures the open and flush directly and keeps codec input conversion and scratch writes under the codec span. The repeated Canon CPU Proofs opened one 216 MB finished strip file and spent about 0.10–0.15 s opening or flushing it. The encoder writes about 216 MB Canon or 509 MB Sony of input planes per JPEG pass, before JPEG data and reads. A cold Proof adds about 360 MB Canon or 848 MB Sony of endpoint and comparison inputs on disk. These are logical file sizes. The traces combine cached reads, mapping faults and buffered writes with computation.

A full-size Proof click builds the delivered JPEG. Once it exists, the backend decodes its endpoints and the browser requests both the HDR and SDR comparisons. Their reconstructions and AVIF encodes run one at a time to stay inside the memory budget. The second wait includes the first request's work, so use the elapsed span through the slower response rather than adding the two HTTP durations. The browser also preloads the delivered JPEG and both comparison files, then presents the chosen view. A normal **Build/Refresh Proof** click passes `force: true`, which rebuilds the JPEG even for the same grade. Existing Proof caches can serve a non-forced revisit and reuse the decoded artifact and comparison pictures by content identity. ## Whole-app memory and the Sony GPU refusal

**The deferred check completed for 10 minutes 10 seconds.** With automatic selection, Canon Fine Grain built a native CPU Proof, then switched among the HDR, SDR and delivered views six times. It peaked at **1.958 GB**, **2.042 GB below** the 4 GB limit, about 30 seconds into gain-map encoding. The encoder helper accounted for **0.821 GB**, the main Python process **0.358 GB** and the Electron GPU helper **0.306 GB**. After the first minute, the highest review sample was **1.529 GB**, and the final sample was **1.328 GB**. macOS stayed at normal pressure and swap went from **1,127 to 1,103 MiB**. This completed review checks one fresh build and sustained inspection. The separate attempts below check successive builds.

During the continuous Canon attempt, three full proofs finished and the highest complete action sampled **2.519 GB**. On that peak the native Ultra HDR helper used **0.821 GB**, Python **0.799 GB**, and the Electron GPU helper **0.356 GB**. This occurred inside gain-map encoding. macOS reported memory pressure during the fourth build, so the watch stopped it. A second, paced attempt finished two builds and stopped during the third after about **7 minutes**, again on OS pressure. The sampled peak for a completed run and the pressure limit for the entire machine measure different things.

New Sony Proof attempts stopped after a system warning, once with swap growth. In the Sony trial export attempt the GPU guard chose CPU immediately. The trace got through CPU Denoise and the HDR render, then stopped during SDR finishing. The last clean samples reached **2.144 GB** for the app tree, with **1.318 GB** in the main Python process. That is a lower bound for an interrupted run. The prior completed Sony runs sampled **3.32–3.39 GB** under the 4 GB cap. Their raw process lists and the new pressure stops are in the evidence folder.

The native Sony image has **42,389,760 pixels**. The trial's first host admission check charges **508,677,120 bytes** for one float RGB source copy, **301,989,888 bytes** fixed (288 MiB), and **3,221,621,760 bytes** for the **76 bytes per pixel** delivery allowance. That is **4.032 GB of additional estimated demand**. The starting charge is the greater of **1.30 GB** and the live whole-app footprint plus reservations. No authored reference or raw sensor payload was present on these delivery requests. The floor alone makes the request **5.332 GB**, at least **1.332 GB over** the 4.000 GB ceiling. The host sees the larger number and uses bounded CPU strips before opening a GPU delivery job. This is a refusal based on the reservation formula. A Sony GPU peak was never observed. [First host check](../../../codebase/backend/hdr_finisher/exporters.py) · [GPU broker check](../../../codebase/backend/hdr_finisher/gpu_delivery.py) · [Memory policy](../../../codebase/backend/hdr_finisher/app_memory.py)

The current input handoff duplicates the developed source. The 32-row readback bounds each individual transfer, while the broker still accumulates two full RGBA32F endpoints (1.356 GB on Sony). It then copies both into full RGB arrays (another 1.017 GB). The hidden renderer separately budgets full-frame finishing textures. Even with sharpening off, its scratch reservation is 32 bytes per pixel plus 32 MiB on this image, or 1.390 GB, before the creative graph. The 80% finishing reservation rule alone requires a hidden-renderer budget of about 1.74 GB at that size. These describe buffer ownership and reservation checks. They overlap in time and are already represented in parts of the guard, so adding them to the guard would double count. [GPU delivery renderer](../../../codebase/frontend/gpu-delivery.js) · [GPU finishing](../../../codebase/frontend/gpu-delivery-finishing.js)

At a representative observed starting charge of **1.60–1.70 GB**, the trial would need to shed **1.63–1.73 GB** from its host estimate. With the current source copy and fixed allowance, the pixel allowance would have to drop from 76 to roughly **35–38 bytes per pixel**. Meeting that budget means streaming the developed source and finished rows to a bounded encoder input, using bounded finishing surfaces, retiring lane-specific creative buffers between renditions, and avoiding the two complete RGBA-to-RGB copies. Check the hidden renderer's independent room for creative scratch and remeasure the real physical footprint. Lowering the estimate by itself would allow allocation without creating that room.

## Measurement notes

MacBook Neo, Apple A18 Pro, six CPU cores, 8 GB unified memory, macOS 27.0.1 on AC power. Unpackaged Electron at 1408 × 794 points. Source development used the signed Bayer path, AHD, lens correction off, and harsh-speck cleanup at 2.5. The cases used matching HDR and generated-SDR Denoise controls, original dimensions, no output sharpening and no metadata. JPEG Ultra HDR used quality 90, full gain map and 1,000-nit Highlight Compression. Proof targeted 1,000 nits. Exposure was 0 EV except the labeled +0.015 EV checks, which made a fresh Proof artifact and could change the number of codec passes.

The clock starts on the Export or Build Proof button and stops at a saved file with the success UI, or at the sharp decoded Proof after two browser frames. Export path selection was automated. Import and settings changes happened before the clock. Memory is the sampled sum of Apple physical footprints for Electron, its GPU/renderer processes, Python and helpers, in decimal GB. macOS memory pressure and swap were sampled roughly once a second. Every app measurement was run inside the shared GPU lock. The 5% comparison band separates ordinary timing spread from cases where another repeat would help.

The locked attempts used about **29 minutes 37 seconds** including setup, stopped runs and the completed review. The hard OS stop took precedence over getting one warm-up and two timed runs in every case. Fresh Sony full-action repeats, the completed Canon Large GPU pair, the repeated Canon Large CPU pair and the stand-alone SDR timing were cut. The single SDR attempt stopped on memory pressure with swap growth. SDR JPEG is structurally different because it builds one rendition and skips the HDR gain map and decoded HDR check, so it needs a timing of its own. The earlier delivered-file timings are carried forward separately. The supplemental Sony backend-only run got through RAW open and then stopped on OS pressure. An early Sony driver attempt also caught an edit-revision race in the external driver. It was corrected by waiting for the grade save before clicking Proof.

The Canon CPU Large export and Proof matched byte for byte. Three 0 EV Canon Fine Proof builds and the two 0 EV Canon Clean Small Proof builds also repeated their respective JPEG hashes. The Canon and Sony originals matched the approved test-photo SHA-256 values after the runs. Total local scratch at the end was **15.9 GB** against the 100 GB limit.

No production files changed. The external driver uses the regular Electron app and its native Export and Proof controls, and directs the save dialog to disposable output. The Python timing observer lives in the ignored evidence folder and uses the existing `[library]` logging format via the same test-only `sitecustomize` mechanism the repository already uses for finished-pixel capture. It records timestamps and preserves original function inputs and results. The GPU observations use the existing delivery timing fields and read-only counters on the test renderer. The input RAWs were read from the approved set through disposable working copies.
