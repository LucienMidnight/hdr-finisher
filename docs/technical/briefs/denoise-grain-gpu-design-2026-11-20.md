# Fine Grain and Clean Grain on WebGPU

November 20, 2026 · Feasibility study · MacBook Neo, Apple A18 Pro, six CPU cores, 8 GB unified memory

## Recommendation for Steve

Fine Grain and Clean Grain can use the same graphics-card drawing path as Standard. At 110% the app can work on the part of the photo you can see and fill in new areas as you pan. The successful path skips the full-photo CPU preparation and its temporary disk file, about 288 MB for the Canon and 678 MB for the Sony. Fine Grain is the first step. Clean Grain can follow the same design, though Grain Evenness in Clean Grain also changes what the colour cleanup sees, so that control may settle shortly after the drag.

Fit shows the whole photo. Keeping today's look at Fit calls for running over the photo at its original size in strips and shrinking the result on the graphics card. Today's grain character also needs one shared fit from the whole photo. The study found visible differences when that fit used Standard's sampling grid. Export and Proof can share the GPU processing with Grade, with the existing CPU version available when admission or execution fails. The Sony is the delivery memory test that matters.

Allow **7–11 engineering days** in three phases. Start with Fine Grain and measure the actual wait in Grade before committing to the remaining work.

## Where the current wait goes

The November 20 A/B work estimated **8.32 s Canon / 15.87 s Sony** from selecting Fine Grain at 110% to the exact picture. It applied the measured CPU preparation savings to the earlier whole-app timings. One newer real-app observation was **4.42 / 9.65 s**, with **0.61 / 0.68 s** for Standard and **3.92 / 7.12 s** for switching from Fine to Clean. These single observations give a stricter target. Aim for about **2.2 / 4.8 s or less** for Fine Grain at 110%, then verify with paired action-to-picture runs. This study uses the existing 5% timing band. [Performance table](../performance-table.md) · [Single-run receipt](../../../codebase/output/denoise-exact-speed-2026-11-20/app-110.log)

The largest CPU calculation is the wavelet. Before the native-filter speedups, Fine Grain's six workers spent a cumulative **17.78 / 48.26 s** on wavelets, **3.01 / 8.40 s** writing scratch and **2.14 / 9.03 s** waiting for the writer. Those worker times overlap. In the native-filter trial, the wavelet totals fell to **5.72 / 14.82 s** and write-lock waits rose to **6.55 / 12.88 s**. The older whole-preparation GPU estimate, about **8 / 24 s**, still paid to pack, read back and write the whole photo. Native GPU drawing can remove that chain and reuse the source pixels already needed for Grade. [CPU profile](../../../codebase/output/perf-audit-2026-11-20/profile.log) · [November 19 estimate](denoise-speed-memory-gpu-prep-2026-11-19.md)

The audit's cheaper CPU alternative filtered only 24 of 117 Canon work units and 28 of 247 Sony work units. Its combined visible-only Fine timings were **0.65 / 0.79 s**, plus **0.21 / 0.55 s** for the fit, starting with developed pixels and excluding app transport and presentation. That makes it a candidate for improving fallback. Live controls would still need repeated CPU processing and a handoff. The proposed GPU route reuses the adaptive compute passes that already exist. ## What moves to the GPU

| Step | Current result and proposed compute | Spatial scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard noise measurement | Reuse the current GPU measurement and small model, with brightness/variance and per-band calibration. Use native-scale source windows for a native Grain recipe and the existing authored-SDR source handling. | Shared per source and geometry. The large-frame GPU measurement uses sixteen 256 × 256 windows. |
| Grain fit | For Y bands 1 and 2, sum four band-power terms per band. If P is squared band signal, g the usual retention gain, and m its 18-pixel local mean, accumulate P, Pg², Pm, and Pm². Those sums give two scale/offset pairs that preserve the original retained grain energy. Reduce within GPU workgroups, then reduce the partials and solve for the constants. The CPU sums in float64. Use pairwise FP32 reduction with conditioning and range/finite checks. Summing Pm(1−m) and P(1−m)² directly also avoids subtracting nearly equal totals in the solve. A tiny statistics readback and scalar solve is available if precision requires it. | One fit per photo and relevant Y settings. The exact current fit uses all pixels. Use a bounded full-photo reduction for the first implementation. Representative windows with halo are an optimization to qualify. |
| Wavelet filtering | Five undecimated, separable B3 scales in opponent colour. The local noise estimate and 7 × 7 band energy set how much of each band to retain. Reuse the existing adaptive WebGPU shader code, tile scratch and selector. | Local. Coarsest cumulative B3 reach is 62 px plus radius 3 for energy. Keep the established 66 px source border. |
| 18-pixel grain matching | Average the Y gains of bands 1 and 2 in a separable 37 × 37 box. Apply the global scale/offset, clamp to [0, 1], then interpolate with the original gain using Grain Evenness. The existing shaders have the gain, box and accumulate passes. Cache the summed original and matched contribution or two scene RGB endpoints for the current region. | Local once calibrated. Worst reach of the fitted bands is 35 px, inside the 66 px source border. |
| Small colour pull | Start with resolved Fine Grain. Work in opponent colour relative to local light and grain. Compare paired samples in four directions at reach 12 with tap radius 2. A close match on one side protects real colour. Local darkness, texture and a 41 × 41 highlight max limit the pull. Retain the 7 × 7 compact-peak test and the per-channel 5 × 5 median fringe step. Use separable boxes/max, a fixed median selection, and a per-pixel shader for the pairs and gates. | Local. Keep the existing 40 px downstream border on the resolved Fine Grain values. |
| Large colour pull | Apply Small first, then the reach-28 pull with tap radius 4 to the Small-corrected chroma. Reuse the original light/noise/texture/highlight guides, as the CPU does. Cache Small output or correction and compute Large when selected or admitted during idle time. Store the final signed chroma delta. Apply Color Splotches as a blend and compensate for the luminance of the delta to preserve scene Y. | Local. Keep the existing 76 px downstream border for the colour chain. Combined source border is 106 px for Small and 142 px for Large. |
| Even out harsh specks | RAW development clamps some Bayer outliers and protects compact lights when red, green and blue peaks agree within a 3 × 3 sensor neighbourhood. Carry the same signed development and protection into Denoise. A change to the RAW control replaces the source and invalidates Grain data. The removed Dark Specks search stays outside this pipeline. | Upstream of Denoise. All three methods receive the same developed source. RAW cleanup has its own noise estimate and sensor coordinates. |

Wavelet reflection distinguishes a real frame edge from the edge of temporary tile storage. Energy and gain boxes replicate. Colour pairs use reflect-101, and the boxes, median and maximum have their own border rules. Pass the geometry-adjusted frame dimensions into every kernel, process the halo, and publish only the core. A tile grid anchored to the frame lets pans reuse pixels. One shared fit prevents a patch from changing its grain as it crosses a tile boundary. Test overlap at alternate tile origins, cropped/rotated images, negative shadows, real highlights and frame corners. ## Interaction, delivery and ownership

- **Fit and first zoom.** Use the shared Grade selector. The fidelity route streams native tiles through the same Grain kernels and reduces to a small Fit surface with the current range-limited resize. At 110% resolve the padded visible rectangle and keep the last complete picture mounted. Reuse the existing compositor and a small GPU Fit underlayer for newly exposed areas. Seed it from a previous Fit draw where available. A concurrently produced exact underlayer can settle later, with the sharp foreground taking priority.
- **Grain Evenness.** The fit stays valid for this control. Fine Grain's interpolation is linear in scene RGB with fixed base settings. Retain endpoints or a base plus signed difference and blend before the grade. At Fit the range clamp and grade can be nonlinear. Blend before those operations or update the provisional result when the exact bounded reduction completes. Clean Grain's guides and pulls depend nonlinearly on the Fine input. Show the current Fine response promptly, coalesce colour work to the latest input, and run the colour region during or shortly after the drag. Interpolated Clean pictures would need their own validation.
- **Color Splotches and Splotch Size.** Color Splotches is already a live delta weight. Size is a two-position Small/Large control. When the Small result and guides are ready, calculate the wide pass only for the selected region. Swap immediately if the selected delta is already resident. Size needs the same grain fit in both positions. Use the exact final selection for export and Proof.
- **Other Denoise controls.** Amount, Luminance, Detail Recovery and the size multipliers for bands 1 and 2 can change the fit. Color Noise, the other band multipliers, Evenness and the colour controls keep the same sums. Cache sampled band data if it earns admission, or run short, cancellable reductions. For the other weight changes, reuse region evidence within budget and run the necessary compute. Grade Exposure and other downstream controls can reuse the latest denoised source. Coalesce obsolete revisions before allocating, yield between small batches, and discard any result whose source or controls changed before the queue finished.
- **Export and Proof.** Run the same native compute and boundary rules in the hidden GPU renderer. Reuse work for HDR and SDR where source, geometry and Denoise settings match. Authored SDR may have different pixels. Deliver full-size images through bounded tiles or strips and keep the finishing, watermark, encoder and final HDR limiter in their current order. Share the full-size render and admission decision between export and Proof. Derive reduced Proof at its existing stage. The delivery transport and Proof decode have separate memory costs.

On GPU success, remove the native prepare/read/release round trips, the packed RGB/half-colour file and its reservation/write-lock/expiry handling, and the prepared-region upload and blend route. Replace the scratch-token Fit underlayer and its retargeting with the ordinary GPU underlayer and viewport compositor. The separate Fine-to-Clean scratch reuse and HDR-to-generated-SDR scratch refresh keys become redundant. Keep identities for source generation, developed/geometry-fixed pixels, rendition when genuinely different, noise model, scale, fitted controls, region and colour reach. Publish only valid, current work. [Preparation ownership](../../../codebase/backend/hdr_finisher/denoise_measurement.py) · [Viewport presentation](../../../codebase/frontend/preview-pipeline.js) · [Shared delivery renderer](../../../codebase/frontend/gpu-delivery.js)

Keep CPU Fine and Clean, including the full-photo fit, Small/Large math, tiling and bounded delivery. Keep any preparation/token/scratch code that still has a CPU or Library caller until that caller has an equivalent fallback or is retired. Missing GPU, unsupported size, compile/validation failure, invalid result or insufficient job capacity goes to the CPU after at most one GPU attempt. A second GPU attempt is for an identified temporary external condition such as preemption, a restarting device or contention. Stay on the last valid picture until the fallback or replacement is ready. Preserve Standard's pixels and use its existing regression coverage. ### Can windows replace the whole-photo grain fit?

On these photos, a fixed grid would change the result beyond tolerance. The CPU sampling test used the exact fit arithmetic with native 256 × 256 cores and a 35 px halo. It compared Standard's 4 × 4 window locations and a denser 8 × 8 grid to the whole-photo fit, holding the Standard noise model fixed. The 16 cores represent about 5.8% of the Canon and 2.5% of the Sony. The 64 cores represent about 23% and 10%.

The evaluation used 31 Canon and 30 Sony patches per setting, including Steve's marked review areas, at Grade Exposure of 0 and +3 EV. It covered default Fine Grain and Clean Grain at both reaches, plus Fine Grain with strong settings (Amount and Luminance 100%, Detail Recovery 0%) and light settings (40%, 35%, 85%). Each table cell is the worst patch mean and p99 across all five cases and both exposures. The two maxima may come from different patches.

| MacBook Neo photo | Fit windows | HDR ΔE ITP mean / p99 | SDR ΔE 2000 mean / p99 | Within both limits? |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Canon | 4 × 4 | 18.478 / 154.964 | 1.882 / 8.511 | No |
| Canon | 8 × 8 | 0.878 / 7.499 | 0.084 / 0.395 | No |
| Sony | 4 × 4 | 0.730 / 8.375 | 0.377 / 1.732 | No |
| Sony | 8 × 8 | 0.331 / 1.103 | 0.086 / 0.308 | Yes |

The denser grid passed the default Fine Grain mean/p99 on both photos. Under stronger settings the Canon still had an HDR p99 of 7.50. On the Sony the 16-window fit at those settings produced zero scale and offset for both bands, while the full fit left offsets of about 0.019 and 0.091. The Sony's denser grid also just missed the tighter delivery tail check in that stress case (HDR p99.9 of 3.023). Even the Canon's default Fine Grain result with the denser grid had a lifted-shadow HDR p99.9 of 8.77.

Band energy can be concentrated in small lights, edges or textured patches. Window statistics cannot recover samples outside their coverage. Ship the exact grain calibration first, as a streamed GPU reduction over the frame, storing only band scratch and partial sums. The Standard noise model can still use bounded samples. The whole-photo reduction is the only analysis pass in Grain that needs to see the entire native frame for today's look. At 110% the reconstruction and colour passes still run only in and around the viewport. A faster fit can be tested later with stratified windows and wider sampling when the subsets disagree, against the same four-RAW, lifted-shadow and marked-count checks. Fit adds native filtering streamed into a small output surface. Cold tracks with the shader pipelines unprepared can add about a second. RAW import and lens/sensor redevelopment are separate waits.

During a pan the compositor should move immediately and show its underlayer where a sharp tile is missing. An uncached jump or other expensive Grade edits can stretch the sharp wait. Other Denoise controls which change the fit can give provisional feedback and settle after the reduction. At Fit, Fine Evenness can provide roughly 50–150 ms provisional feedback from the fitted endpoints. An exact update can require another native reduction if the endpoint blend misses after range limiting. Clean Evenness or an uncached Size setting can take seconds for an exact Fit update. Computing both Size deltas on an admitted idle native pass allows a quick later swap. Cached Size results and Color Splotches use the small Fit surface.

The 110% projection starts from Standard's measured 0.61 / 0.68 s whole wait and allows for the ~0.13 s Grain reconstruction, shared source transport/draw and colour work. It also includes **0.4–1.1 s Canon / 0.9–2.3 s Sony** for the exact fit, estimated from a narrower two-band brightness calculation, the measured full wavelet runtime and source transport. One resident fit avoids this cost for an unchanged recipe. Prefetch only when an active Grain recipe and the broker permit it. The estimate comfortably halves the 8.3 / 15.9 s baseline. The upper Canon range crosses half of the 4.42 s single observation, so measure that case before committing to a production route.

Fit starts from the measured 0.87 + 0.41 s and 2.04 + 0.93 s compute/upload totals and adds the exact fit, resize, Grade and presentation. Clean adds an unmeasured colour pass and the wider halo. Export uses the earlier whole-app Standard delivery of 12.57 / 38.67 s as an anchor and allows for the native passes, lane handoffs and memory-bounded scheduling. The Sony Standard reading used bounded CPU delivery, so it is a size and codec anchor. Earlier measured Fine/Clean delivery was 27.76 / 34.10 s Canon and 45.56 / 52.84 s Sony, with CPU work and different memory conditions. The export estimates assume native JPEG Ultra HDR quality 90 and both renditions. CPU fallback, a heavier grade, a distinct authored SDR base or another codec can change them. The likely return is in Grade responsiveness. A halved full-export wait still needs evidence. [Whole-app and delivery baselines](../performance-table.md)

## In plain language

Yes, the graphics card can do the heavy calculation, and the two test photos matched the current look. At 110% plan for **about 1–3 seconds on the Canon and 2–4½ seconds on the Sony** for the first sharp Grain picture. At Fit, keeping the same character takes **about 2–5 and 4–10 seconds**. Expect quick slider feedback, with Clean Grain sometimes taking a few tenths of a second longer to settle at 110%. The exact Fit picture can take longer to catch up. Full-size JPEG export is projected at **14–24 seconds Canon and 32–54 seconds Sony** when the graphics-card job fits safely.

I recommend **2–3 days for Fine Grain first**, then check it on the Neo before spending the rest of the **7–11 days**. Use the whole photo to measure grain, work on the visible patch at zoom, and keep the CPU ready as a backup. Bring Clean Grain and delivery over once the first step proves the wait and the look in the real app.
