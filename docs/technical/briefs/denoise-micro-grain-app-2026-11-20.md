# Micro Grain in HDR Finisher — November 20, 2026

Steve chose Micro Grain as the fourth Denoise method on November 20. The work reached the Grade feasibility check. Its reference is the signed-Bayer round 3 recipe `balance-0.7-L0.2-C0.7`. The research comparator uses lens correction off and the optional ×2.5 harsh-speck setting. In the app that RAW setting remains independent of Denoise. The method needs an eligible Bayer RAW source. [Decision](../../product/modules/denoise.md#3-steves-decisions) · [Round 3](../research/denoise-v1-2026-11-16/predemosaic-round3.md)

## Grade and delivery design

Use the existing per-phase noise fit from before RAW cleanup. Take Micro Grain's Bayer input after RAW cleanup and sensor highlight reconstruction. Separate the packed-Bayer measurements and correction coefficients from slider-dependent reconstruction. They depend on the source, so identical HDR and generated SDR grades can share them. Measure the adaptive noise model on the unfiltered development as round 3 does. Preserve signed samples and round to sensor codes before demosaicing. Reuse the Library WebGPU AHD arithmetic for bounded tiles and the existing adaptive wavelet and Small colour repair for the late steps. First qualify that GPU path in an isolated viewport probe. The Library's whole-frame GPU allocations are unsuitable for direct reuse in the 4 GB Grade budget.

Prioritize padded native tiles under the viewer. At Fit, process native strips into a reduced output. Coalesce slider input to the latest value, keeping the previous valid image visible as new tiles arrive. If exact filtering during a drag misses the response target, qualify a GPU blend of exact local slider samples during a drag, followed by an exact render on release. Signed AHD's direction votes and the adaptive wavelet gains are nonlinear, so measure blend error on finished pixels before choosing it.

Keep the settled CPU reference bounded to the same native core and haloes. Export and full-size Proof use the shared CPU result in the existing finishing and ceiling paths. Retain the normal bypass and Show original selection of the unfiltered source. On a permanent GPU failure, fall back once to CPU. Admit source, scratch and retained tiles against the existing budgets.

## Controls and qualification

Use Luminance, Color and Detail with 50% defaults. Luminance scales the round 3 early 0.70 and late 0.20 strengths. Color scales early 0.70, adaptive 1.20× and Small repair 1.00×. Detail adjusts the existing structure gate and first late-band retention around the fixed recipe in a narrow range. Check its minimum and maximum on the same flat and hair patches, including the change in remaining flat noise and edge contrast relative to the default. The design adds no flat-only stage.

For a warm drag, target a first visible update within 150 ms. This covers the existing Clean Grain 110–137 ms colour drag and leaves roughly one frame of headroom. For settled Grade, use the existing same-machine 5% speed band for repeated measurements and compare the full release wait against Standard and Clean Grain in the same session. Require HDR ΔE ITP and SDR ΔE 2000 mean <1 and p99 <3, the exact pre-encode HDR ceiling and the existing decoded allowance, and a peak below 4 GB for Grade, export and Proof. Differences from the round 3 default may be up to 0.05 px in grain size, 0.03× Clean Grain in chroma RMS, 0.10 percentage points in mean brightness and 0.10 ΔE 2000 in the smoothed skin measurement. Those bounds preserve the published texture, tone and colour results. Stop before offering the method if neither Grade path can deliver live feedback at acceptable cost. ## Feasibility result

The signed AHD demosaic is fast enough for the visible patch once its Bayer input is resident. The prototype reused the Library's WebGPU arithmetic and shared the Lab calculations between neighbouring pixels. Its warm median was 8.9 ms on the Canon and 7.1 ms on the Sony. At +3 EV the isolated GPU demosaic and CPU demosaic agreed within both finished-pixel gates. The late stage ran the five adaptive bands, centred their corrections, ran the existing GPU Small repair and centred that correction too. Its finished-pixel comparison at +3 EV also passed on both photos. These are separate stage checks.

The complete late stage alone took 255–260 ms with 512-pixel tiles and 202–203 ms with 1280-pixel tiles at native zoom. The larger tiles raised logical GPU allocations from 144 MB to 364 MB. The 1024-pixel run with Color at zero skipped the Small repair and took 117–120 ms before Bayer work, AHD, Grade finishing and presentation. At Fit, repeating the native pass over the approximate frame area took 1.78 s Canon and 4.42 s Sony with 512-pixel tiles. This estimates just the late compute. The isolated CPU round 3 viewport updates took 0.94–1.22 s Canon and 1.04–1.27 s Sony after initial Bayer tile preparation. [Timing conditions](../performance-table.md#november-20--micro-grain-grade-feasibility)

During-drag interpolation between exact slider samples missed the finished-pixel gates. With samples only five slider percentage points apart, a midpoint in Canon skin and face detail near default Luminance reached HDR ΔE ITP 1.21 mean / 18.66 p99. The same crop at half size was 1.07 / 12.24. The Canon dark patch near minimum Color reached 11.20 / 180.58 in HDR and 1.70 / 24.76 in SDR. A dense cache would also carry a startup and memory cost. For example, caching three axes at five-point intervals around the current setting would take up to 61 renders and about 1.2 GB for one 2.49 MP viewport at eight bytes per finished pixel. This estimate excludes the active render graph and the source. Moving a different slider invalidates the other axes' samples.

At the 50% defaults the CPU port measured 3.30 / 3.39 / 3.30 px grain in the three Canon flats. Their mottling was 0.28× / 0.27× / 0.23× Clean Grain, and mean brightness changed by −0.99% / −0.04% / −0.74% relative to Off. Canon forehead colour was ΔE 2000 0.251, with a −0.26° hue change. Sony flats measured 3.36–3.41 px grain, 0.21–0.68× mottling and −0.08% to +0.08% brightness change. The worst finished CPU port comparison to the research recipe across the measured crops was HDR ΔE ITP 0.00064 mean / 0.0053 p99 and SDR ΔE 2000 0.00012 mean / 0.0014 p99. The stricter scene-pixel tolerance failed in 880 Canon face channels and 322 Sony flat channels. The port would need that resolved if adopted as the reference.

The full Detail range stays close to round 3's protection. Relative to 50% Detail, at 0% the RMS of the brightness correction changed by +0.4% to +1.9% across the seven flat patches and +2.5% to +6.5% across hair, face and lashes. At 100% it changed by −1.0% to −0.7% across the flats and by 0.0% to +1.7% across those texture patches. The prototype used the round 3 brightness bands and did not add the round 4 flat-area stage.

Both attempts to run Standard and Clean Grain together in Grade were stopped by macOS memory pressure during app startup, including the retry from normal pressure. The earlier same-machine Clean Grain colour drags took 123–137 ms to reach the settled view at 110%. The newer Micro Grain measurements cover isolated compute. A same-session action-to-picture comparison remains open. [Clean Grain Grade readings](../performance-table.md#november-20--clean-grain-gpu-trial-in-grade)

## Decision at the stop gate

The live Grade requirement remains open. The fastest exact candidate exceeds the provisional 150 ms target before the rest of the pipeline runs. The tested interpolation fails on lifted shadows at native and half size. Kept the method out of the app menu and left the existing Denoise controls available. No app delivery, Library, GPU failure injection, save/restore or whole-app memory check for Micro Grain was possible at this stage.

There are two implementation options. Lower-traffic GPU wavelet and repair passes would need to cut the current 202–203 ms viewport compute enough to leave time for Bayer preparation and the Grade draw. A release-only trial could start from the roughly one-second CPU viewport update, with a different expectation for slider response. With either option, use the round 3 research recipe as the delivery reference and reconcile the CPU port's remaining scene differences.
