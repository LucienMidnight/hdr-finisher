# Micro Grain in HDR Finisher — November 20, 2026

Steve chose Micro Grain as the fourth Denoise method on November 20, and directed us to build the live GPU route after feasibility. Its reference is the signed-Bayer round 3 recipe `balance-0.7-L0.2-C0.7`. The research comparator uses lens correction off and the optional ×2.5 harsh-speck setting. In the app that RAW setting remains independent of Denoise. The method needs an eligible Bayer RAW source. [Decision](../../product/modules/denoise.md#3-steves-decisions) · [Round 3](../research/denoise-v1-2026-11-16/predemosaic-round3.md)

## Grade and delivery design

Use the existing per-phase noise fit from before RAW cleanup. Take Micro Grain's Bayer input after RAW cleanup and sensor highlight reconstruction. Separate the packed-Bayer measurements and correction coefficients from slider-dependent reconstruction. They depend on the source, so identical HDR and generated SDR grades can share them. Measure the adaptive noise model on the unfiltered development as round 3 does. Preserve signed samples and round to sensor codes before demosaicing. Reuse the Library WebGPU AHD arithmetic for bounded tiles and the existing adaptive wavelet and Small colour repair for the late steps. First qualify that GPU path in an isolated viewport probe. The Library's whole-frame GPU allocations are unsuitable for direct reuse in the 4 GB Grade budget.

Prioritize padded native tiles under the viewer. At Fit, draw a reduced approximation, then process native strips into an exact reduced output. Coalesce slider input to the latest value, keeping the previous valid image visible while the update runs. Do not blend local slider samples: signed AHD's direction votes and the adaptive wavelet gains are nonlinear, and the tested blend missed the colour gates.

Keep the settled CPU reference bounded to the same native core and haloes. Export and full-size Proof use the shared CPU result in the existing finishing and ceiling paths. Retain the normal bypass and Show original selection of the unfiltered source. On a permanent GPU failure, fall back once to CPU. Admit source, scratch and retained tiles against the existing budgets.

### Implementation

The app keeps the corrected Bayer region and structure evidence separate from Grade's output. The backend measures the native source and returns bounded phase-aligned regions. Grade groups adjacent tiles into Bayer bands where they fit the working budget. It uses signed WebGPU AHD and applies the adaptive wavelet, centred Small colour repair and RAW calibration on the GPU. Fit uses a reduced scene-wavelet approximation first, then native strips. Exact tiles and Fit reductions can be reused across matching HDR and generated SDR. Library small pictures use the scene-wavelet approximation. CPU previews and delivery use the bounded CPU implementation. Crops and applied lens correction use the CPU; DNG, odd Bayer bounds, Edge Colour highlights, retouch repairs and authored SDR are not supported by this method.

The production CPU crop check reproduced 3.30–3.41 px brightness grain and a Canon skin measurement of 0.251 ΔE 2000. The Canon face had 880 scene-channel differences above the reference tolerance; the Sony mid check had 322. Worst finished disagreements against round 3 were HDR ΔE ITP 0.00064 mean / 0.0053 p99 and SDR ΔE 2000 0.00012 mean / 0.0014 p99 across the tested crops. These are CPU/reference crop results, not Grade-versus-delivery results.

A Canon Grade check injected one GPU failure. The status reported the CPU switch and the viewer presented a denoised 4096-pixel CPU preview, with no second Micro Grain GPU attempt. In a separate smoke check, the Library drew the 640-pixel developed picture from a saved Micro Grain grade, and Grade restored the enabled method and custom Color value on reopen. The Library CPU fallback can omit Micro Grain because it has no Bayer data.

## Controls and qualification

Use Luminance, Color and Detail with 50% defaults. Luminance scales the round 3 early 0.70 and late 0.20 strengths. Color scales early 0.70, adaptive 1.20× and Small repair 1.00×. Detail adjusts the existing structure gate and first late-band retention around the fixed recipe in a narrow range. Check its minimum and maximum on the same flat and hair patches, including the change in remaining flat noise and edge contrast relative to the default. The design adds no flat-only stage.

The feasibility target was 150 ms for a warm slider update. Steve made it a target, not a gate, and approved live GPU rendering on the basis of the roughly 0.25–0.30 s isolated probe. The integrated picture wait is longer; see the [actual app timings](../performance-table.md#november-20--micro-grain-in-grade-and-delivery). The qualification gates remain HDR ΔE ITP and SDR ΔE 2000 mean <1 and p99 <3, the exact pre-encode HDR ceiling and the existing decoded allowance, and a peak below 4 GB for Grade, export and Proof. Differences from the round 3 default may be up to 0.05 px in grain size, 0.03× Clean Grain in chroma RMS, 0.10 percentage points in mean brightness and 0.10 ΔE 2000 in the smoothed skin measurement.

## Feasibility result

The signed AHD demosaic is fast enough for the visible patch once its Bayer input is resident. The prototype reused the Library's WebGPU arithmetic and shared the Lab calculations between neighbouring pixels. Its warm median was 8.9 ms on the Canon and 7.1 ms on the Sony. At +3 EV the isolated GPU demosaic and CPU demosaic agreed within both finished-pixel gates. The late stage ran the five adaptive bands, centred their corrections, ran the existing GPU Small repair and centred that correction too. Its finished-pixel comparison at +3 EV also passed on both photos. These are separate stage checks.

The complete late stage alone took 255–260 ms with 512-pixel tiles and 202–203 ms with 1280-pixel tiles at native zoom. The larger tiles raised logical GPU allocations from 144 MB to 364 MB. The 1024-pixel run with Color at zero skipped the Small repair and took 117–120 ms before Bayer work, AHD, Grade finishing and presentation. At Fit, repeating the native pass over the approximate frame area took 1.78 s Canon and 4.42 s Sony with 512-pixel tiles. This estimates just the late compute. The isolated CPU round 3 viewport updates took 0.94–1.22 s Canon and 1.04–1.27 s Sony after initial Bayer tile preparation. [Timing conditions](../performance-table.md#november-20--micro-grain-grade-feasibility)

During-drag interpolation between exact slider samples missed the finished-pixel gates. With samples only five slider percentage points apart, a midpoint in Canon skin and face detail near default Luminance reached HDR ΔE ITP 1.21 mean / 18.66 p99. The same crop at half size was 1.07 / 12.24. The Canon dark patch near minimum Color reached 11.20 / 180.58 in HDR and 1.70 / 24.76 in SDR. A dense cache would also carry a startup and memory cost. For example, caching three axes at five-point intervals around the current setting would take up to 61 renders and about 1.2 GB for one 2.49 MP viewport at eight bytes per finished pixel. This estimate excludes the active render graph and the source. Moving a different slider invalidates the other axes' samples.

At the 50% defaults the CPU port measured 3.30 / 3.39 / 3.30 px grain in the three Canon flats. Their mottling was 0.28× / 0.27× / 0.23× Clean Grain, and mean brightness changed by −0.99% / −0.04% / −0.74% relative to Off. Canon forehead colour was ΔE 2000 0.251, with a −0.26° hue change. Sony flats measured 3.36–3.41 px grain, 0.21–0.68× mottling and −0.08% to +0.08% brightness change. The worst finished CPU port comparison to the research recipe across the measured crops was HDR ΔE ITP 0.00064 mean / 0.0053 p99 and SDR ΔE 2000 0.00012 mean / 0.0014 p99. The stricter scene-pixel tolerance failed in 880 Canon face channels and 322 Sony flat channels. The port would need that resolved if adopted as the reference.

The full Detail range stays close to round 3's protection. Relative to 50% Detail, at 0% the RMS of the brightness correction changed by +0.4% to +1.9% across the seven flat patches and +2.5% to +6.5% across hair, face and lashes. At 100% it changed by −1.0% to −0.7% across the flats and by 0.0% to +1.7% across those texture patches. The prototype used the round 3 brightness bands and did not add the round 4 flat-area stage.

The initial feasibility attempts to run Standard and Clean Grain together in Grade stopped on macOS memory pressure during startup. The later production run completed the same-session Canon comparison: Micro Grain's 110% slider update took 1.26 s after Bayer tile grouping (1.76 s before), versus 0.05 s for Clean Grain and 0.46 s for Standard on the measured actions. The Sony session completed Micro Grain before pressure stopped the comparison run. [Production measurements](../performance-table.md#november-20--micro-grain-in-grade-and-delivery)

On both RAWs, CPU export and full-size Proof produced byte-identical Ultra HDR JPEGs. Whole-app peak memory was 2.09 / 2.68 / 2.62 GB for Canon Grade / export / Proof and 2.81 / 3.49 / 3.48 GB for Sony. The export changes for Micro Grain are limited to the Denoise source hook and CPU route selection; the codec, finishing and ceiling paths are shared and unchanged by this method.

Finished 110% Grade viewport comparisons to the CPU delivery pixels passed the Micro Grain gates on both RAWs. Canon measured HDR ΔE ITP 0.149 mean / 2.277 p99 and SDR ΔE 2000 0.173 / 0.523. Sony measured HDR 0.095 / 0.166 and SDR 0.245 / 0.622. Canon HDR p99.9 was 9.646; the specified tail gate is p99. At the 1,000-nit setting, the native pre-encode peaks were 1,000.000 nits Canon and 465.456 nits Sony (already below the ceiling). Matching HDR and generated SDR Grade views reported the same prepared Bayer source key on both RAWs. Sony used separate Grade and CPU exporter captures after the combined capture stopped on system memory pressure; both finished pixel arrays were written before the diagnostic encoder stopped. The separate normal Sony app export and Proof completed and matched byte for byte.

The final locked Node suite passed 1,127 tests with one skip. The monolithic Python retry stopped on memory pressure at 71%; the complete Python suite then finished under the same lock in seven fresh groups (4,363 passed, 150 skipped, one unexpected pass, no failures).

## Historical feasibility stop

Feasibility stopped with the method outside the app menu. The fastest exact candidate exceeded the provisional target before the rest of the pipeline ran. The tested interpolation failed on lifted shadows at native and half size. Steve subsequently directed that the GPU route be built with live sliders and no release-only mode.

Lower-traffic GPU wavelet and repair passes remain a possible speed improvement. No additional filter tuning or flat-area-only stage was included.
